The best places to retire in the world are not interchangeable. Greece and Portugal offer European residence routes for people with independent income; Panama and Costa Rica have explicit pension-based categories; Thailand has a recognisable over-50 route; and Malaysia suits applicants who can tie up substantial capital. The right answer depends on whether you prioritise a low entry threshold, specialist healthcare, English, tax simplicity, climate or a route that can support long-term residence.

This guide is written for international retirees, including Americans comparing retirement abroad. Immigration rules were checked on 29 October 2026. Eligibility, financial evidence and the correct consulate depend on nationality and legal residence, so verify the current checklist before transferring money, buying property or ending health coverage at home.

How to compare the best places to retire abroad

Many lists begin with weather and rent. Those matter, but they do not answer whether you can remain legally, reach the right hospital or keep enough money liquid after deposits and property requirements. Compare every country using the same six questions:

  1. Can you qualify for a durable residence route? Check income, pension, age, deposits, dependants, permitted work and renewal rules.
  2. What must remain liquid? Separate monthly spending from application costs, refundable deposits and compulsory property purchases.
  3. Can you obtain appropriate healthcare? Look at the exact city and specialist network, not only a national ranking.
  4. What triggers tax residence? Immigration residence and tax residence are different. Obtain country-specific tax advice before moving investments or pensions.
  5. Will normal life work? Test transport, banking, language, groceries and medical appointments outside the holiday district.
  6. What is the exit plan? Know how quickly you can end a lease, recover deposits and move if health, visa rules or exchange rates change.

Panama and Costa Rica: the clearest pension-based options

Panama

Panama is one of the strongest overseas retirement locations for people with a documented lifetime pension. The Embassy of Panama states that Pensionado applicants must apply in Panama through a Panamanian lawyer and show at least USD 1,000 a month, plus USD 250 for each dependant. Documents such as pension proof, marriage records and police checks must be prepared in the required form.

Panama City offers the broadest hospitals and flight network. Boquete has a cooler highland climate and a substantial international community, but complex medical care may still require travel. Coastal areas can be attractive, yet heat, humidity and driving time change the daily experience.

Best for: retirees who value a purpose-built pension route and convenient connections to North America. Think twice if: your pension is temporary rather than lifelong, or if you are choosing a smaller town without checking specialist-care access.

Costa Rica

Costa Rica’s official consular guidance describes Pensionado residence for applicants who can document a lifelong pension of at least USD 1,000 per month. Foreign civil and pension documents may need apostilles and translations. Residence also creates local healthcare and compliance obligations that should be priced before the move.

The Central Valley offers the most practical access to major hospitals and a milder climate. Beach communities provide a different lifestyle but can mean higher imported-goods costs, seasonal roads and longer travel for complex care.

Best for: people who want a defined pension category and value nature more than low urban costs. Think twice if: your budget assumes that every popular beach town is inexpensive.

Greece and Portugal: strong European choices with different friction

Greece

Greece is attractive because it combines a Mediterranean lifestyle with a residence category for financially independent applicants. Official regional guidance requires a national Type D visa, private insurance and evidence of stable means without employment in Greece. Published figures and consular practice have changed, so the current income amount must be confirmed with the mission responsible for your legal residence.

Athens and Thessaloniki offer the deepest hospital and transport networks. Kalamata and Crete may deliver a slower pace, but island or regional life can add flight and ferry dependence. A picturesque village is not automatically a practical base for someone who needs regular specialist care.

Best for: independently funded retirees who want Europe and can manage local administration. Think twice if: the plan depends on employment or on living far from medical services.

Portugal

Portugal’s government portal provides a residence-visa service specifically for retirees, religious applicants and people living on their own income. The application is handled through the consular system and requires the documents applicable to the applicant’s residence and circumstances. It is a real residence route, but it should not be reduced to a single income number copied from a blog.

Porto and the Lisbon region have broad services but significant housing pressure. Smaller cities can reduce rent, while the Algarve offers a large international community and seasonal demand. Confirm healthcare access, year-round transport and rental availability in the exact municipality.

Best for: retirees seeking European infrastructure with documented recurring income. Think twice if: the budget relies on pre-2020 housing prices or an obsolete tax incentive.

Spain, Italy and France: viable, but not low-friction retirement visas

Spain offers a non-working residence visa for people with sufficient guaranteed means. Official 2026 consular guidance uses 400% of IPREM for the principal applicant and requires qualifying health insurance. The route does not permit gainful work and some consulates explicitly exclude remote work. Spain is strongest for retirees who value infrastructure and climate more than tax simplicity.

Italy uses the Elective Residence visa for applicants with substantial, stable income independent of employment. Italian consular guidance commonly cites pensions, annuities, property income and investments, together with long-term accommodation. Published consular starting points are around EUR 31,000–32,000 per applicant, but the decision is qualitative and submitting the minimum does not guarantee approval.

France has a long-stay visitor route rather than a branded retirement visa. France-Visas requires applicants to demonstrate their socio-economic situation, resources, accommodation and medical cover and to agree not to work. France can be a strong healthcare and transport choice, but it is rarely the cheapest retirement destination.

Mexico: excellent location choice, consulate-specific financial proof

Mexico is one of the most practical retirement countries for Americans because it offers short flights, varied climates and cities ranging from Mérida and Oaxaca to Puerto Vallarta and San Miguel de Allende. The immigration path is less uniform than a simple nationwide dollar threshold suggests.

Mexican consulates publish temporary- and permanent-resident requirements using economic-solvency formulas that change with official units and consular jurisdiction. Some permanent-residence routes are limited to retirees or pensioners. Apply through the mission responsible for where you legally live and use its current checklist; do not assume that a threshold quoted by another consulate will be accepted.

Best for: North American retirees who want proximity and many city types. Think twice if: you have not checked the exact consular threshold, local security pattern and private-healthcare options for the chosen city.

Thailand and Malaysia: Asia’s strongest entries, with very different capital requirements

Thailand

Thailand remains one of the best retirement destinations for people who want strong value, established international communities and private hospitals. Chiang Mai, Hua Hin, Bangkok and Phuket suit different budgets and climates, so the country should not be treated as one uniform lifestyle.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes the Non-Immigrant O-A route for applicants aged 50+. It permits a stay of up to one year and does not allow employment. The published financial test is THB 800,000 in a bank deposit, THB 65,000 monthly income or an allowed combination, together with applicable insurance and reporting obligations.

Malaysia

Malaysia stands out for English, private healthcare and infrastructure. Penang is attractive for food, hospitals and a more compact urban rhythm; Kuala Lumpur has the broadest specialist care and international connections.

The federal MM2H programme now has Silver, Gold and Platinum categories. The official portal lists fixed deposits of USD 150,000, USD 500,000 and USD 1 million, renewable terms of five, 15 and 20 years, and compulsory property purchases beginning at RM 600,000, RM 1 million and RM 2 million. For applicants aged 50+, the current overview shows no annual minimum-stay requirement.

Which retirement country should you shortlist?

  • Choose Panama if a clear lifetime-pension route and air links matter most.
  • Choose Costa Rica if nature and institutional stability outweigh the need for the lowest costs.
  • Choose Greece or Portugal if European residence, walkable cities and regional travel are priorities.
  • Choose Mexico if proximity to the United States and a wide range of climates matter, after checking your consulate’s solvency rules.
  • Choose Thailand for the strongest balance of value, hospitals and destination variety in Asia.
  • Choose Malaysia for English and private healthcare if the deposit and property rules fit your balance sheet.
  • Choose Spain, Italy or France if the European lifestyle is worth higher proof-of-means and tax-planning complexity.

Reduce the list to two or three countries, verify the residence route for your passport and legal residence, and spend at least one normal month in each candidate city. Test healthcare, transport and housing outside peak season before making an irreversible move.

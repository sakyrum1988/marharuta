Driving and Transport in Asia: Licences, Safety and City Choice

Short answer: do not assume that a licence valid at home lets you drive anywhere in Asia. A visitor may need the physical home licence plus the correct International Driving Permit, while a resident may have to convert or obtain a local licence.

Before renting a car or scooter, verify the rule with the destination's transport authority and check that the vehicle class, insurance and your immigration status all match.

For many moves, the better decision is not how to drive but whether you need to drive at all. Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Hong Kong and central Taipei can support a car-free routine. Bali, Phuket, provincial Thailand and many smaller cities create a very different transport problem.

An IDP is normally a translation and standardised companion document, not a replacement for the original licence. The convention accepted by the destination matters. Japan, for example, recognises qualifying IDPs issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention and requires the original paper document; a phone image is not enough.

Visitor rules and resident rules are different

Short-stay guidance is often written for tourists. Once you receive a residence or work status, a different clock may begin. Singapore allows most foreigners residing for less than 12 months to use a valid foreign licence with an IDP or an official English translation, but residents staying longer generally need a Singapore licence. New permanent residents and citizens have a shorter conversion window.

Japan uses another structure: a qualifying IDP or a limited group of foreign licences with an approved Japanese translation can be used for up to one year after entry, subject to document validity. Repeated exits do not automatically restart every licensing entitlement. These differences are why a regional table can only be a screening tool.

  • Check the rule for your passport and the country that issued your licence.
  • Check whether the destination accepts the 1949 or 1968 convention document.
  • Check whether your licence class explicitly covers motorcycles.
  • Check the resident conversion deadline before the move, not after buying a vehicle.

Do a seven-day mobility test before signing a lease. Travel to the workplace or coworking space at peak time, test the grocery run, hospital route and airport connection, and repeat one journey in heavy rain. A cheap apartment can become expensive when every trip requires a taxi or a second vehicle.

Why scooters create the largest hidden risk

Scooters are cheap, available and woven into daily life across Southeast Asia. That convenience hides three separate questions: whether the rider is legally licensed, whether the rental vehicle is insured and whether the policy will pay after an accident. A car licence does not automatically authorise a motorcycle. A rental shop handing over keys does not prove legality.

The World Health Organization reports that more than half of global road deaths involve vulnerable road users such as pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists. In its South-East Asia regional work, WHO also highlights the region's disproportionate road-injury burden. Treat a helmet, closed shoes, sober riding and avoiding night or monsoon travel as basic controls, not optional lifestyle choices.

Before riding, photograph the vehicle, tyres, brakes and existing damage. Confirm the excess, theft conditions, authorised drivers and emergency number in writing. If the rental agreement excludes unpaved roads, flooding or an unlicensed rider, a cheap daily rate can expose you to the full cost of the vehicle and third-party damage.

Build the real monthly transport budget

Compare the complete mobility cost, not fuel alone. For a private vehicle include purchase or rental, deposit, registration, insurance, parking, tolls, fuel or charging, maintenance, tyres, inspections and depreciation. For a transit-first plan include monthly passes, taxis after the last train, airport trips and occasional car rental.

Families should price the school run separately. A home that saves $150 in rent but requires two daily taxi journeys can be the more expensive option. Use the cost calculator for a normal month and the budget planner for deposits, licence conversion and vehicle setup.

Accident checklist for a foreign driver

  1. Move only when necessary for immediate safety and call the local emergency number.
  2. Do not admit liability in a language or document you do not understand.
  3. Photograph positions, plates, damage, road conditions and relevant signs.
  4. Contact the rental company and insurer through the number in the policy.
  5. Request a police or official incident record when the policy requires it.
  6. Keep medical records, receipts and every message about the claim.

Store offline copies of the passport identity page, visa, home licence, IDP, rental agreement and insurance certificate. Do not leave the only originals inside a parked vehicle.

Decision: when driving is worth it

Drive when the legal documents are clear, the route materially improves daily life and the insurance remains valid for the exact vehicle. Stay transit-first when a city has strong public transport, parking is difficult or you would be learning unfamiliar traffic conditions at the same time as adapting to a new country.

Frequently asked questions

Is an IDP valid everywhere in Asia?

No. Recognition depends on the destination, the convention and issuing authority, the original licence and the vehicle class.

Can I ride a scooter with a car licence?

Do not assume so. Verify the motorcycle category on both the home licence and IDP and check local law and insurance.

Should I buy a vehicle immediately after moving?

Usually not. Test the neighbourhood and normal routes first, then price the complete ownership cost and conversion requirements.

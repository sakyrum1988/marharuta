Studying in Asia

Student visas in Asia: admission, work rights and costs

A student visa begins with admission to a recognised institution. It does not guarantee unrestricted work, dependant rights or an automatic switch to employment after graduation.

Quick answer

Choose the programme and verify that the institution can support foreign students. Then align admission and visa timing, proof of funds, insurance, housing and work rules. Do not pay a non-refundable amount until the refusal and refund policy is clear.

Seven checks before filing

  1. Recognition: is the institution accepted by immigration?
  2. Admission: is an unconditional offer or eligibility certificate required?
  3. Funds: what amount and which sponsor are accepted?
  4. Language: which test and validity period apply?
  5. Insurance: is a local scheme mandatory and when does it start?
  6. Work: what hours are permitted during term and holidays?
  7. Family: can dependants join and may a spouse work?

First-year budget

  • application and enrolment fees;
  • tuition and compulsory materials;
  • visa, legalisation and translations;
  • insurance and medical screening;
  • dormitory or rental deposit;
  • flight, transport and connectivity;
  • a reserve that does not depend on part-time work.

Work permission may require a separate approval and should not be the only plan for paying tuition.

After graduation

Check whether a job-search period exists, whether status must change before employment begins and which employer can sponsor it. Study years may count differently toward permanent residence. Treat study, work and settlement as three separate legal stages.

Red flags

  • a visa is promised without genuine admission;
  • the institution is absent from the official list;
  • unlimited work is promised on student status;
  • refund terms after refusal are hidden;
  • fake financial or attendance documents are suggested.

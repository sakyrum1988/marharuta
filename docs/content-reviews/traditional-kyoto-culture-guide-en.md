# Editorial review: traditional-kyoto-culture-guide (EN)

## Scope and audience

- Reader task: choose meaningful cultural experiences in Kyoto, group them into a realistic itinerary and avoid behaviour that harms residents, workers or heritage sites.
- Audience: English-speaking international visitors; nationality is not assumed.
- Language and search market: English, Google US.
- Research date: 2026-10-30.
- Competitor URL from the editorial queue: `https://internationalliving.com/discovering-traditional-japan-in-the-city-of-kyoto/`.
- Format: practical culture and itinerary guide, not a first-person relocation story.
- Status: **Ready in local dev; not published to production**.

## Competitor review

| Page | Strength | Gap addressed in our article |
|---|---|---|
| International Living, Discovering Traditional Japan in Kyoto | Strong atmosphere and a detailed first-person year of study in tea, arts and meditation | Personal essay offers little booking logic, crowd planning or current official etiquette; its experience is not attributed to Margarita |
| Klook, Best Cultural Experiences in Kyoto | Clear attraction and activity categories with booking-oriented comparisons | Commercial framing and volatile prices; our article avoids unverified price promises and gives an ethics-first decision framework |
| Cooking Sun, Kyoto Traditions | Introduces lesser-known traditions and festivals | Old, short and promotional; limited logistics, source verification and visitor conduct |
| Study Kyoto, Kyoto Culture | Good overview of tea, flower arranging, zazen, clothing and crafts | Catalogue format; our article turns categories into area choices, verification questions and a two-day plan |
| Kyoto Travel, Activities and Responsible Travel | Current official activity directory and explicit etiquette | Information is spread across several pages; our article connects it into one reader journey while linking back to the official pages |

Additional value: an experience-selection table, area-by-area planning, tea-ceremony questions, explicit Gion conduct, crowd-aware transport advice, a two-day route and a Kyoto-shi terminology note.

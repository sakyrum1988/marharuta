<div class="tvm-section" id="visas">
<h2><span class="icon">🛂</span> Visa &amp; Residency Comparison <span class="tvm-winner-tag my">Malaysia Wins</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand Visa Options</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>LTR Visa</strong> — 10 years, for wealthy globals, retirees, remote workers, specialists ($80k+ income or $250k savings)</li>
<li><strong>Thailand Elite</strong> — 5–20 years, membership fee ($15,000–$30,000), no income proof</li>
<li><strong>Retirement Visa</strong> — 1 year renewable, age 50+, ฿800,000 in Thai bank (~$22,000)</li>
<li><strong>SMART Visa</strong> — for startups and investors, 4 years</li>
<li><strong>Education Visa</strong> — flexible option, requires enrollment in school</li>
<li><strong>Tourist Visa</strong> — 60 days + 30-day extension, easy to obtain</li>
<li><em>No easy permanent residency path</em></li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia Visa Options</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)</strong> — 5–10 years, renewable; income RM40,000+/mo or RM1.5M assets — best long-term visa in SEA</li>
<li><strong>DE Rantau (Digital Nomad)</strong> — 12 months + 12-month extension, income $24,000+/year, approx. $1,000 fee</li>
<li><strong>Retirement Visa</strong> — 10 years via MM2H Silver for 60+</li>
<li><strong>Employment Pass</strong> — for those with job offer in Malaysia</li>
<li><strong>Tourist Visa</strong> — 30–90 days visa-free for most Western passports</li>
<li><em>PR possible after 2–5 years of MM2H</em></li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on visas:</strong> Malaysia wins clearly. The MM2H is widely regarded as the best structured long-term residency program in Southeast Asia, with a genuine path toward permanent residency. Thailand has more variety but less stability — visa rules have changed frequently.</p>
</div>
<div class="tvm-section" id="healthcare">
<h2><span class="icon">🏥</span> Healthcare Comparison <span class="tvm-winner-tag th">Thailand Wins</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand Healthcare</h3>
<ul>
<li>Bangkok home to <strong>Bumrungrad International</strong> — one of Asia&#8217;s top hospitals</li>
<li>JCI-accredited facilities widely available</li>
<li>Medical tourism hub — high standards for international patients</li>
<li>GP visit: $20–50 | Specialist: $50–120</li>
<li>International health insurance: $1,200–3,000/year</li>
<li>Dental: world-class at 30–50% of Western prices</li>
<li>Expat-friendly hospitals in Chiang Mai, Phuket, Koh Samui</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia Healthcare</h3>
<ul>
<li>Excellent private hospitals — Pantai, Gleneagles, KPJ networks</li>
<li>Significantly cheaper than Thailand for equivalent quality</li>
<li>GP visit: $15–35 | Specialist: $40–100</li>
<li>International health insurance: $900–2,500/year</li>
<li>Strong public health system for residents (low cost)</li>
<li>Penang known as Malaysia&#8217;s healthcare hub</li>
<li>English-speaking doctors universally</li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on healthcare:</strong> Thailand wins on absolute quality (Bumrungrad is world-class) but Malaysia wins on value — equivalent quality at lower prices, and English-speaking doctors across the board. For most expats, Malaysia&#8217;s private healthcare is more than adequate and cheaper.</p>

<div class="tvm-section" id="lifestyle">
<h2><span class="icon">🌴</span> Lifestyle &amp; Culture <span class="tvm-winner-tag th">Thailand Wins</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand Lifestyle</h3>
<ul>
<li>Vibrant nightlife — Bangkok, Phuket, Pattaya, Koh Samui</li>
<li>World&#8217;s best street food scene</li>
<li>Buddhist culture — temples, meditation retreats, wellness</li>
<li>Beaches: Krabi, Koh Lanta, Koh Tao — some of Asia&#8217;s best</li>
<li>Strong nomad and expat party scene (Chiang Mai, Bangkok)</li>
<li>Massage culture — $8–15 per hour massages everywhere</li>
<li>More relaxed social atmosphere</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia Lifestyle</h3>
<ul>
<li>Multicultural — Malay, Chinese, Indian cultures coexist</li>
<li>Excellent food diversity (Penang ranked among world&#8217;s best food cities)</li>
<li>More conservative — limited alcohol, dress codes in some areas</li>
<li>Good nightlife in KL&#8217;s KLCC and Bangsar areas</li>
<li>Nature: rainforests, Cameron Highlands, Borneo (Sabah/Sarawak)</li>
<li>Less beach-centric than Thailand</li>
<li>Family-friendly, stable, quieter pace</li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on lifestyle:</strong> Thailand wins for vibrancy, nightlife, beaches, and pure fun. Malaysia wins for cultural diversity, food complexity, and family-friendliness. Choose based on your priorities.</p>

<div class="tvm-section" id="safety">
<h2><span class="icon">🛡️</span> Safety &amp; Crime <span class="tvm-winner-tag my">Malaysia Slight Edge</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand</h3>
<ul>
<li>Generally very safe for expats</li>
<li>Petty theft in tourist areas (Koh San Road, Pattaya)</li>
<li>Scams targeting tourists common (tuk-tuk, gem, temple scams)</li>
<li>Road safety is a concern — motorbike accidents high</li>
<li>Political protests occasionally affect Bangkok</li>
<li>Personal safety: 90th percentile globally</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia</h3>
<ul>
<li>Very safe — consistently ranks in top 30 safest countries</li>
<li>Snatch theft a known issue in some KL areas</li>
<li>Road safety: KL highways can be dangerous</li>
<li>Politically stable, no major protest history</li>
<li>Strong rule of law, low corruption</li>
<li>Personal safety: 85th percentile globally</li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on safety:</strong> Both are safe for expats. Malaysia has a slight edge on institutional stability. Thailand has more tourist scams but violent crime is rare. Both are far safer than most Western urban areas.</p>
</div>
<div class="tvm-section" id="language">
<h2><span class="icon">🗣️</span> Language &amp; English <span class="tvm-winner-tag my">Malaysia Wins Clearly</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand</h3>
<ul>
<li>Thai is the national language</li>
<li>English spoken in tourist areas, international hotels, malls</li>
<li>Limited English in local markets, government offices</li>
<li>Learning Thai basic phrases helps significantly</li>
<li>Thai script on signs, menus outside tourist zones</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia</h3>
<ul>
<li>English is a <em>de facto</em> official language — used in business, government, courts</li>
<li>Virtually everyone in urban areas speaks fluent English</li>
<li>Government forms and official processes available in English</li>
<li>Banking, healthcare, schools — all English-capable</li>
<li>No language barrier for daily life</li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on language:</strong> Malaysia wins convincingly. English is functionally the working language of Malaysia. For expats who don&#8217;t want to learn a local language, this is a major advantage — especially for banking, healthcare, and dealing with bureaucracy.</p>
</div>
<div class="tvm-section" id="family">
<h2><span class="icon">👨‍👩‍👧</span> Families &amp; International Schools <span class="tvm-winner-tag my">Malaysia Wins</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">

<div class="tvm-section" id="climate">
<h2><span class="icon">☀️</span> Climate &amp; Weather <span class="tvm-winner-tag tie">Personal Preference</span></h2>
<div class="tvm-compare-grid">
<div class="tvm-country-card th">
<h3>🇹🇭 Thailand</h3>
<ul>
<li>Tropical — hot, humid year-round</li>
<li>Rainy season: May–October (heavy in south)</li>
<li>Cool season (Nov–Feb): pleasant 25–30°C in north</li>
<li>Chiang Mai smoke season: Feb–April (air quality poor)</li>
<li>More beach/island diversity than Malaysia</li>
<li>No typhoon risk</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="tvm-country-card my">
<h3>🇲🇾 Malaysia</h3>
<ul>
<li>Tropical — hot, humid year-round (28–35°C)</li>
<li>Two monsoon seasons: northeast and southwest</li>
<li>KL rains almost daily (short, heavy showers)</li>
<li>Cameron Highlands: cool retreat at 1,500m (15–25°C)</li>
<li>No extreme heat spikes or smoke seasons</li>
<li>No typhoon risk</li>
</ul></div>
</p></div>
<p><strong>Verdict on climate:</strong> Very similar — both are tropical and humid. Thailand has a distinct cool season that many find pleasant. Malaysia is more consistently hot with no smoke season. Personal preference determines the winner here.</p>

<div class="tvm-faq" id="faq">
<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<div class="tvm-faq-item">
<h3>Is Thailand or Malaysia cheaper to live in?</h3>
<p>Malaysia is generally 10–20% cheaper than Thailand for a comparable lifestyle, particularly when comparing Kuala Lumpur to Bangkok. However, Chiang Mai and Penang are roughly similar in cost. Malaysia wins on rent, dining, and healthcare costs. Thailand is cheaper for nightlife in some contexts (cheaper beer, more street food options).</p>
</p></div>
<div class="tvm-faq-item">
<h3>Which has the better visa — Thailand or Malaysia?</h3>
<p>Malaysia wins clearly on long-term visa stability. The MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home) offers 5–10 years of renewable residency with a genuine path to permanent residency. Thailand has more variety (Elite, LTR, retirement) but the rules have changed frequently, and there is no straightforward path to PR or citizenship.</p>
</p></div>
<div class="tvm-faq-item">
<h3>Is Malaysia or Thailand better for digital nomads?</h3>
<p>Thailand (specifically Chiang Mai and Bangkok) is better for digital nomads due to the size and maturity of the nomad community, coworking scene, and overall infrastructure built around remote workers. Malaysia launched the DE Rantau nomad visa and is catching up, but Thailand remains the regional leader for nomad culture.</p>
</p></div>
<div class="tvm-faq-item">
<h3>Which country is better for families — Thailand or Malaysia?</h3>
<p>Malaysia is better for families. International schools are cheaper ($8,000–20,000/year vs $15,000–30,000 in Bangkok), English is used as the language of instruction throughout, and the overall family infrastructure is stronger and more widespread. Malaysia also has a slight safety and stability advantage.</p>
</p></div>
<div class="tvm-faq-item">
<h3>Can I move between Thailand and Malaysia to avoid visa runs?</h3>
<p>Yes — many expats use both countries strategically. Thailand and Malaysia share a land border (at several crossing points including Padang Besar and Bukit Kayu Hitam), making border runs straightforward. Some long-term travellers split time between both — enjoying Thailand&#8217;s lifestyle while using Malaysia as a base for visa resets.</p>
</p></div>
